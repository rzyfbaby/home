Fireplace Room Sofa Plan

Your room already has a strong base: the fireplace wall, a window full of plants, and two tall lamps on the long wall that look like they're waiting for a sofa to stand between them. These are three ways to lay it out, plus sofas you can get near Surrey without overspending.

What I read from your photos
  • Fireplace wallTiled gas fireplace inside white panelled millwork with an arched niche. This is the focal point.
  • Window wallA tall window with a fiddle-leaf fig, monstera, dracaena and a row of potted plants. Don't block it.
  • Long blank wallTwo lamps on iron stands, with the grey velvet chairs, side table and a monstera between them.
  • Open sideOpens to the front entry and hallway beside the white pillar, so people walk through here.
  • What's already greyThe shag rug, velvet chairs, wicker chairs and floor cushions are all grey. A warm or green sofa will balance them better than another grey one.
  • Small rugThe current rug is about 5×8 ft. It floats in the middle. An 8×10 would pull the seating together (optional).

The plans below assume a room of about 14 × 17 ft, estimated from the photos. They're drawn to that scale.

Three layouts

Recommended

A · Sofa between the lamps

Put the sofa on the long blank wall, centred between the two lamp stands. The stands become your end tables. The wicker chairs face it from the plant side, and the velvet chairs close the group at the entry end.

Good

  • Lamps and outlets are already there
  • Window and plants stay open
  • Walkway from the entry stays clear
  • Uses every chair you own

Watch

  • Sofa sits side-on to the fireplace
  • Sofa must fit the lamp gap (≤ ~85 in)
  • Monstera moves to a corner
Best size: 79–85 in, not too deepIKEA Uppland · Structube Teresa
Most cozy

B · Facing the fire

Float the sofa in the middle of the room, facing the fireplace with its back to the entry. Chairs face each other in pairs on either side. This is the classic fireplace layout, and the sofa back acts as a soft wall between the room and the hallway.

Good

  • Everyone faces the fireplace
  • Makes the room feel separate from the entry
  • A longer, deeper sofa fits here

Watch

  • Needs about 3 ft of walkway behind the sofa
  • Sofa back is on show (pick one finished on all sides)
  • A lamp or plant has to move behind it
Best size: 84–90 in, deep seatIKEA Kivik · Brick Tula
Lowest cost

C · Loveseat and a plant nook

A loveseat goes between the lamps and the velvet chairs face it across the coffee table. The wicker chairs and a small round table move into the corner by the window to make a tea and reading nook among the plants. Your current rug still works here.

Good

  • Cheapest: a loveseat is about $600
  • Keeps the current rug
  • Gives the plant corner a purpose

Watch

  • Seats 2 on the sofa (6 in total)
  • Not ideal for lying down
Best size: 60–70 in loveseatIKEA Uppland loveseat

Colour to choose

The room already has grey in the rug, chairs and cushions, plus dark walnut and a lot of green. A sofa in one of these colours will tie it together instead of adding more grey.

Oatmeal / light beigeThe safest choice. It brightens the room and makes the walnut table stand out. Uppland, Kivik.
Deep greenEchoes the fig and monstera. It's bold, and it hides wear well. Ceni, Teresa (Green).
Cognac / rustMatches the copper fireplace tile and the Moroccan lanterns. A warm, cozy option.
Grey (use with care)Only choose grey if you add warm pillows and throws. Otherwise the room starts to look flat.
